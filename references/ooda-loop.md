# The OODA loop — how Battle Buddy decides your next move

*No volatile figures in this file. It explains a decision method, not benefits.*

OODA — **Observe, Orient, Decide, Act** — came from Air Force Colonel John Boyd. It's a real
decision framework built for moving fast under uncertainty, which is exactly the situation you're
in when the structure that used to assign your tasks is gone (or hasn't started yet).

`/next-move` is one honest turn of this loop. Here's what each step means here.

## Observe — get the real picture

Read what's actually true: your stage, your mission, your open loops, your deadlines, your ground
truth. No wishful thinking, no pretending a constraint isn't there. Garbage in the Observe step
poisons everything after it.

## Orient — make sense of it

This is the step people skip, and it's the one that matters most. Orient means interpreting what
you observed *against your specific situation*:

- What does your **stage** change about what's urgent? (A pre-enlistment "next move" and a
  veteran "next move" are not the same species of thing.)
- What's the **real deadline** — verified live from an official source, not guessed?
- What's the **highest-leverage** thing — the one that unlocks others?
- What are you **avoiding** because it's uncomfortable rather than unimportant?

## Decide — pick one

Not a list. One next action, chosen because it moves the mission more than anything else you
could do right now. A good decision is small enough to start today and clear enough that you know
when it's done.

## Act — make it real

Battle Buddy doesn't stop at "you should update your résumé." It drafts the thing, sets up the
step, writes the email for your review. Act closes the loop — and the result becomes the first
thing you Observe next time.

## Why a loop, not a plan

Plans assume the world holds still. It doesn't — a claim gets denied, a job posting appears, a
ship date moves. OODA is built to re-run: you Act, the situation changes, you Observe the new
situation, and you go again. Speed through the loop beats a perfect plan you made once and never
revisited. That's the whole idea.
