# The OPORD framework — why this system is shaped the way it is

*No volatile figures in this file. It explains structure, not benefits.*

If you've served, you've read an operations order. The five-paragraph OPORD is how the military
organizes any mission so that anyone can pick it up and know what's going on. Battle Buddy borrows
that structure on purpose — because it's a thinking tool you already own, and because your life
after (or before) service is, in fact, an operation you're running.

## The five paragraphs → the five layers

**1. Situation** → `situation/`
What's true on the ground. Your stage, your record, who you are out of uniform, the real
constraints. In an OPORD this is enemy/friendly/terrain; here it's *your* terrain — the honest
picture everything else has to work with.

**2. Mission** → `mission/`
The who-what-when-where-why in one sentence. If you can't recite it, it's not a mission yet.
Everything downstream is measured against this.

**3. Execution** → `.claude/skills/`
How the work actually gets done — the concept of operations and the tasks. In Battle Buddy, your
capabilities: the skills that turn intent into a finished thing.

**4. Sustainment** → `sustainment/`
Logistics and support — what keeps you in the fight. Your benefits, your resources, and the
people in your corner. Under-using this paragraph is the single most common transition mistake.

**5. Command & Signal** → `comms/`
Who's in charge, and how everyone stays synced. Your battle rhythm, your check-ins, and the
systems Battle Buddy can reach.

## Why it helps

- **Nothing hides.** Every piece of your situation has one obvious place to live.
- **You can brief yourself.** Open five folders, know exactly where you stand.
- **It scales down.** A 17-year-old deciding whether to enlist and a retiree with 20 years both
  run the same five paragraphs — only the contents differ.

You don't have to think in OPORD terms to use Battle Buddy. But if you already do, you'll feel at
home — and that's the point.
