# National Guard & Reserve — the part the active-duty model misses

> **Verified:** 2026-07-21 · **Contains no volatile figures.** Guard/Reserve eligibility rules
> hinge on *how* and *how long* someone served on qualifying orders, and those rules change and
> vary by person. This file names how the component works and where the official answer lives.
> Battle Buddy pulls the specifics live and cites them, and routes any claim to a free VSO.

Most transition and benefit guidance quietly assumes an **active-duty separation** — one long tour,
one DD-214, one clean exit. For a Guardsman or Reservist that model is wrong in ways that cost real
money. Don't assume active-duty. Ask about component and orders first.

## The two components

- **Reserve** (Army Reserve, Navy Reserve, Air Force Reserve, Marine Forces Reserve, Coast Guard
  Reserve) — federal force. Typically drills one weekend a month + annual training (the "one
  weekend a month, two weeks a year" shorthand), and can be **mobilized** to active duty.
- **National Guard** (Army National Guard, Air National Guard) — **dual-status**. Normally under
  the *governor* of their state; can be **federalized** under the President. (There is no Navy,
  Marine, Space Force, or Coast Guard National Guard.)

## Title 10 vs Title 32 — the hinge that decides benefits

This is the single most important thing to get right, and the thing to verify rather than guess:

- **Title 10** — federal active-duty orders (mobilization, deployment). This is the kind of service
  that most often **counts toward** federal benefits like the Post-9/11 GI Bill and VA eligibility.
- **Title 32** — federal *pay*, but under **state** control (many Guard missions, some disaster and
  homeland response). Treated differently for some federal benefits than Title 10.
- **State Active Duty** — fully state orders; different rules again.

The days a person spent on **qualifying active-duty orders** are what unlock or size many benefits —
so "how long were you on Title 10 orders?" is often the real question behind "what do I qualify
for?" **Never state the current threshold or tier from memory — verify it live and cite it.**

## Where Guard/Reserve realities change the answer

- **GI Bill.** Eligibility and the *percentage* tier can differ from active duty and often track
  cumulative qualifying active-duty time. There's also a separate **Montgomery GI Bill – Selected
  Reserve (MGIB-SR)**. Which one, and how much — verify on VA.gov, never quote from memory.
  (https://www.va.gov/education/about-gi-bill-benefits/how-to-use-benefits/reserve-national-guard/)
- **VA disability & health care.** A condition connected to a **qualifying period of service** can
  be service-connected for a Guardsman/Reservist too — status alone doesn't disqualify anyone.
  Route the claim to a free accredited VSO. (https://www.va.gov/disability/)
- **Health insurance.** Drilling members may be eligible for **TRICARE Reserve Select**; rules
  change around mobilization. Verify. (https://www.tricare.mil/reserveselect)
- **Retirement.** Guard/Reserve retirement is typically a **points-based** system with pay often
  starting later than active-duty retirement — a different model entirely. Verify current rules.
- **DD-214 vs NGB-22.** A Reservist gets a **DD-214 only for periods of qualifying active duty**;
  Army/Air National Guard service is documented on an **NGB-22**. Someone may hold several, or none
  for their drilling time. This matters — the DD-214 is the key to many benefits.
