# Rank & pay — how the structure works

> **Verified:** 2026-07-21 · **Contains no volatile figures.** Pay tables change every year and
> vary by location. This file explains the *structure* only. For any actual dollar figure, pull
> the current table from DFAS and cite it — never state pay from memory.

## Paygrade vs. rank

Every service member has a **paygrade** (E-1 through E-9, W-1 through W-5, O-1 through O-10) and a
**rank** (the title — Sergeant, Petty Officer, Captain). Paygrade is the pay-and-seniority spine
and is common across branches; rank names differ by branch. When translating to civilians, lead
with what you *did* and how many people/how much budget you were responsible for — not the rank
title, which civilians can't calibrate.

- **Enlisted:** E-1 to E-9.
- **Warrant officers:** W-1 to W-5 (technical specialists).
- **Commissioned officers:** O-1 to O-10.

## How military pay is composed

Total compensation is more than base pay. The pieces:

- **Base pay** — set by paygrade and years of service. Published annually in the military pay
  tables.
- **BAH** (Basic Allowance for Housing) — varies by **location**, paygrade, and dependent status.
- **BAS** (Basic Allowance for Subsistence) — food allowance.
- **Special and incentive pays** — hazardous duty, sea/flight, language, bonuses, etc.
- **The tax-advantaged part** — allowances like BAH/BAS are generally not taxed, which is why a
  military salary doesn't compare one-to-one with a civilian gross figure.
